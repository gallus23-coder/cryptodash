## Summary

- **Candle-to-candle moves rarely clear fees on their own**: pooled daily closes beat Tier 1's 1.3% round trip only 54.3% of the time (6-month window); 4h closes, 21.2%. Trading every move is a losing game before strategy is even considered.
- **Real ≥10%-ish swings are infrequent**: averaging 1.5 swings/month/coin (4h, 10% zigzag threshold, 6-month window) — the opportunity is in catching a handful of trends, not constant trading.
- **Fixing the trend-follower's look-ahead bug changed the picture materially**: the corrected real-time rule (enter +3% off the lowest close since the last exit, trail 5%, one position at a time, 4h candles) nets -1.40%/trade after Tier 1 fees over 134 trades (6-month window), compounding to -90.65% — compare this against the earlier (buggy, zigzag-hindsight) report's numbers, which were optimistic by construction.
- **Filtering entries to only fire when BTC is above its own 200-day EMA** (decided at the prior close, no look-ahead) changes the 4h trend-follower to 72 trades at -3.13%/trade, compounding to -90.97% — see §3 in each window for whether the filter actually helps or just trades less.
- **Buy-and-hold over the 6-month window ranged from ADA at -40.03% to LINK at -4.61%** after one Tier 1 round trip — coin choice and timing both matter far more than fee tier.
- **BTC's 200-day EMA switch rule (now look-ahead-free) returned 2.78%** with 14.68% max drawdown across 2 trades (6-month window) — compare against plain BTC buy-and-hold in §5.
- **Longer windows tell a different story than 6 months alone** — see the Window comparison section: 2-year and 4-year results for the same rules often diverge from the short window, which is itself the main argument for not over-fitting to any single 6-month sample.
- **All 6 coins had full history for every window** (6mo/2yr/4yr) — no "longest available" fallback ever triggered; see each window's §0 header for the per-coin confirmation.

## Methodology notes / known simplifications

- §2 (swing counting) still uses hindsight-confirmed zigzag pivots — that's fine for *describing* how often reversals of a given size happen, but §3's trend-follower trades off a real-time rule instead (lowest-close-since-exit / highest-close-since-entry), with no knowledge of future reversals.
- The EMA baselines and the BTC trend filter both apply a signal decided at the *previous* close to *today's* return/entries — never today's own just-computed signal — closing the look-ahead bug from the first version of this report.
- All simulations use candle **closes** only — no intrabar fills.
- The BTC filter gates new entries only; it does not force an exit if BTC's signal flips while a position from one of the 6 coins is already open.
- The equal-weight basket rebases each coin to its own window-start price and nets one round-trip fee per coin — a dispersion-vs-average comparison, not a tradeable rebalancing strategy.
- "Pooled" compounded return merges every coin's trades into one chronological sequence, as if one capital pool traded all 6 coins' signals.
- **Compounded return assumes 100% of equity is re-risked on every trade** — it is a pure "edge per trade, compounded" figure, not a realistic position-sized backtest. A system with a small negative (or barely positive) edge per trade will compound toward -100% over enough trades at this sizing even though a 0.5%-of-equity-per-trade version of the same signals would survive comfortably. Read the compounded-return column as "does this edge survive being compounded at all", not as a literal account outcome — `netPctPerTrade` and `winRate` are the sizing-independent numbers to trust first.
- Fee tiers are used exactly as given (round-trip totals); no live TradeVolume/fee-tier data is stored in this DB to cross-check against.
